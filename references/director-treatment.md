# Director Treatment and Visual Development

Use this stage when the user needs a richer explanation of how the creative idea should look, sound, and feel, or needs visual prompts for approval.

## Treatment components

Explain why each choice serves the idea and audience:
- Director statement and film promise.
- Emotional arc and performance approach.
- Image style, palette, texture, and atmosphere.
- Camera grammar, lens character, framing, focus, and movement.
- Rhythm, transitions, music, voice-over, sound effects, and silence.
- Character/persona design and continuity.
- World, locations, props, and art direction.
- Product presentation, proof moment and closing image. Include requested brand artwork or end cards.
- Visual development plan and open approval decisions.

Avoid empty style adjectives. Translate them into visible camera, lighting, performance, material, color, and pacing choices.

## Visual reference plan

When a visual development plan is useful, define each asset by the approval question it answers:
- Style frame for tone, color, texture, and atmosphere.
- Camera frame for lens, framing, and camera attitude.
- Beat board or contrast pair for emotional progression.
- Character sheet for consistent casting, wardrobe, makeup, and posture.
- Scene concept for location, time, props, and practical feasibility.
- Product hero frame for shape, material, logo, and interaction.
- Lighting reference for source, shadow, highlight, and color.
- Key visual for the campaign's central image.

Write an image prompt for each requested visual, label its purpose, and keep each asset separate unless the request calls for a beat board or character sheet. Character sheets should show the same person consistently in front, side, and back views, neutral pose, stable wardrobe and proportions, and no celebrity likeness. Product prompts must follow supplied references.

This stage produces the treatment and visual proof. A substantial visual-development package may include approval stills. Generate the requested visuals with a compatible host image tool. For written treatments and prompt-only work, supply the visual plan and prompts. Follow `advanced-visual-assets-and-preproduction.md` and `image-generation-workflow.md`. These references contain methods, not a renderer. Video rendering requires a separate supported tool and an explicit request. Keep generated references separate from final production frames. Preserve exact product and brand artwork.

## Handoff

Give the pre-production or storyboard stage the locked visual system, performance and world rules, product handling, sound direction, reference assets, approval items, and what is still flexible.
