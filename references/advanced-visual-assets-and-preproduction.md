# Advanced Visual Assets and Pre-Production

Use for TVC treatments, visual development, campaign key visuals, or AI production that needs reusable reference assets. For routine social-copy and prompt-only requests, do not add this work unless requested.

## Plan assets from the approved story

Make an asset list from actual scenes and continuity needs. For each asset, state the question it answers, which later shots use it, and whether it is supplied, generated, or still missing.

- **Product anchor:** official product views, proportions, materials, colors, labels, interface, and interaction. Prefer supplied official imagery. Do not invent hidden construction or internal parts.
- **Character anchor:** stable identity, body, wardrobe, hair, grooming, and accessories when the same person recurs.
- **Location/scene anchor:** architecture, surfaces, time, weather, light direction, palette, and practical props.
- **Style frame:** photographic/CG/illustrative direction, contrast, color, grain, and texture.
- **Camera frame:** lens character, shot size, angle, perspective, focus, and movement.
- **Lighting frame:** source, direction, softness, contrast, reflections, and ambient fill.
- **Key visual / hero frame:** campaign's central image, product visibility, copy-safe area, and intended use.
- **Storyboard/grid frames:** chosen story beats with the same locked assets and visual system.

The user's supplied artwork is the source of truth for product shape, logo, labels, and screen. Keep style references, edit targets, product references, and supporting inserts clearly distinguished.

## Default asset sequence

Use the following as a practical order, adjusting when references already exist or the user wants a different route:

1. Confirm product source images and written product truth.
2. Lock the visual direction and the intended output model/format.
3. Build product, character, and environment anchors required by the story.
4. Review the anchors for fidelity and continuity.
5. Create the key visual or storyboard frames from those anchors.
6. Convert approved visual beats into video prompts.
7. Finish the end card and post-production copy/layout separately when exact brand artwork or text is required.

Do not ask for the same product references twice. If the user skips an asset, provide a text description as an assumption or mark that the prompt needs the asset; do not pretend an image exists.

## Visual development in a director treatment

For a substantial treatment, develop the visual proof that helps approve the direction: style frame, camera frame, character/persona, scene, product presentation, lighting/color, and key visual when relevant. When approval images are part of the deliverable, use an available image tool. A text-only treatment includes the visual direction and production prompts. If no renderer is available, deliver the prompts and identify the missing capability without claiming images were made. Generated references remain approval/reference material, not final production footage.

## Asset prompt construction

For each image, identify its purpose and compose the prompt with the still-image method. Use plain visible details:

- product multi-view: consistent scale and orthogonal views, neutral studio background, readable material and silhouette;
- character sheet: same person, front/side/back or requested angles, neutral pose, stable wardrobe and proportions;
- scene concept: establish the empty space, time, surfaces, atmosphere, and light; add people and product later unless their presence is the point of the image;
- product hero: clear silhouette, controlled background, accurate materials, deliberate highlight and shadow;
- material macro: identify the actual surface, scale cue, focal plane, light angle, and texture;
- key visual: one strong read, deliberate product placement, and space reserved for post-produced copy when needed.

These are prompt recipes, not a requirement to fabricate unseen product details. Avoid asking an image model to typeset exact logos, small labels, legal copy, or complex UI. Composite supplied brand artwork and exact text in post when precision matters.

## Continuity anchors and generation notes

Write reusable anchors before generating a sequence:

- product silhouette, visible controls, color/material, logo position, scale, approved states;
- character identity, wardrobe, hair, makeup, posture, allowed expression/action changes;
- location geometry, time of day, weather, palette, key light direction, practical light sources;
- camera language and focal-length feel;
- what may change between frames and what must remain fixed.

For repeated assets, generate distinct prompts for distinct assets; variants are for alternatives of the same asset. Inspect for distorted geometry, altered branding, unapproved UI, identity drift, inconsistent lighting, accidental text, and artifacts. Revise one main variable at a time.

## Pre-production decisions

In a PPM or production pack, cover approved concept and structure, cast/performance, wardrobe/grooming, art direction/props/locations, product preparation and hero handling, camera/lens/movement, lighting/color, sound/VO/music/supers, shot schedule, formats, risks, and client approvals. Distinguish locked choices from references and open decisions. Name the owner and consequence of every unresolved item.

Actual rendering, export, upload, or external model access is not contained in these instructions; it requires a compatible tool in the execution environment.
