# Advanced Film Creative Playbook

Use this route when the user asks for a TVC, brand film, cinematic product film, client-facing creative proposal, or a more developed film treatment. It extends the social-video workflow; it does not override a user's shorter format, approved idea, or stated production limits.

## Entry modes

Choose the shortest route that fits the request:

1. **Full film development:** brief diagnosis → 2–3 creative routes → selected route → treatment/look development → pre-production → storyboard → prompts → production/edit/review as requested.
2. **Asset or prompt only:** use the supplied product truth and brief, state material assumptions, and create only the requested visual asset or prompt.
3. **Storyboard conversion:** retain the approved story and map each shot to a frame, prompt, reference, or video segment.
4. **Revision:** diagnose the actual output issue, revise the affected dimension, and preserve everything already approved.

Do not force a full chain when the user requests one deliverable. When a complete pitch is requested and the direction is not selected, offer distinct routes before expanding one.

## Choose the narrative model

Use the model as a creative decision, not a mandatory template. Each route should express one clear audience truth and one verified product role.

| Model | Story engine | Useful when |
|---|---|---|
| Problem → resolution | Recognizable obstacle is changed by a verified product benefit | A concrete use problem is central |
| Cinematic product reveal | Product form, construction, or verified behavior unfolds in phases | Form, material, or mechanism is the main attraction |
| Brand-world crosscut | Human use world and product-detail world alternate through motivated visual matches | The product is strongly tied to a place, activity, or culture |
| Lifestyle film | Product stays naturally within a real-life scene; camera attention reveals it | Wearable, carried, or everyday-use products |
| Emotional anchor | A relationship, promise, ritual, or memory gives the product meaning | Emotion is credible and supported by the brief |
| Montage reveal | A sequence of visual details builds curiosity before a legible product reveal | A short film needs rapid sensory escalation |
| Before / after | A clear, truthful change in situation or experience is contrasted | The difference can be shown without an unsupported outcome claim |
| Brand manifesto | A point of view or value drives the film; product provides the concrete landing | The brand has an approved position or belief |

## Build the brand world and product role

Think in two connected spaces:

- **Product world:** shape, materials, controls, assembly, verified functions, close detail, and light.
- **Brand world:** the emotional, cultural, or practical context in which the product matters.
- **Bridge:** a motivated match in shape, movement, sound, color, or action that connects the worlds.

Choose the brand-world carrier from the category:

- **Functional products:** the environment or task carries the value (workshop, road, field, lab, outdoors). Show only uses and environments supported by the brief.
- **Identity products:** the person's styling, posture, and behavior carry the value; keep background subordinate.
- **Sensory products:** texture, preparation, application, taste, scent, or sound carries the value. Use accurate, observable material behavior.

Then choose an integration strategy:

- **Product film:** remain in the product world; reveal construction, material, function, and final form in a deliberate arc.
- **Crosscut film:** complete a coherent beat in one world, then bridge to the other. Keep world changes at meaningful beat boundaries rather than jumping randomly inside an action.
- **Lifestyle film:** stay in the lived scene. Let blocking, focus, perspective, and camera movement make the product legible without an unmotivated studio cutaway.

Break these defaults when the chosen idea gives a clear reason.

## Casting and visual translation

Decide whether a person is needed and what the audience must read: face, hands, posture, clothing, interaction, or silhouette. Lock identity, body proportions, wardrobe, grooming, and accessories only to the degree continuity requires. If the image/video model has known reference limitations, verify the target model's current behavior instead of assuming a universal restriction.

Translate abstract benefits into observable film evidence:

- benefit → an action or physical detail the camera can show;
- brand tone → performance, environment, light, color, texture, sound, and pace;
- emotional promise → a visible decision, reaction, gesture, or changed relationship;
- product feature → a verified product state or a clear post-produced graphic, never an invented interface or mechanism.

Design sound alongside the image: location ambience, tactile sounds, silence, designed effects, voice, and music should support the same story beat.

## Pitch and handoff

When pitching alternatives, make each route materially different in insight, story engine, product role, and visual grammar. For the selected route, record:

- project understanding and communication task;
- audience insight and tension;
- one-sentence idea, key message, and approved slogan if any;
- story outline, product role, emotional turn, and memory point;
- key visual, world choice, camera/light/sound direction;
- deliverable versions and open approvals.

Hand the approved route to treatment and storyboard with the confirmed product truth, audience, selected model, visual rules, evidence, mandatories, prohibitions, and unresolved decisions.
