# Creative Direction for Social Content

For a TVC/brand-film proposal, use `advanced-film-creative-playbook.md` to select among the narrative models, choose a product-world/brand-world integration, and define casting and visual translation. Keep those extended film methods optional for routine social posts.

Use this method to turn a verified product profile into a focused campaign idea and a practical visual direction.

## Diagnose the communication task

Identify:
- Who needs to notice or understand the content.
- The recognizable situation, need, or tension in their life.
- What they should understand, feel, remember, or do afterward.
- One product promise supported by a verified fact.
- The evidence or demonstration that makes that promise credible.
- The requested platform, language, format, and call to action.

Keep product truth separate from emotional interpretation. Do not turn a mood or metaphor into an unsupported product claim. Use one main product proof point per short video unless the user asks for a different structure.

## Develop the idea

Write a one-sentence creative premise. For story-led video, establish a protagonist with a concrete goal, an obstacle, a turning point connected to the verified product fact, and a visible or emotional payoff. Make the first three seconds create a clear question, surprise, conflict, or promise that the ending resolves.

Offer multiple creative routes only when the user asks to compare directions or when the brief materially supports distinct approaches. Make each route meaningfully different in audience tension, product role, and story—not just adjectives.

For recurring series, preserve the approved umbrella theme and weekly map. Give each episode a distinct hook, setting, action, and story beat while letting it advance the shared thread.

## Set the director's approach

Translate style words into decisions a production team can execute:
- Point of view, performance, and acting beats.
- Location, time, props, and background behavior.
- Framing, lens character, movement, focus, and edit rhythm.
- Light source, contrast, palette, texture, and atmosphere.
- When and how the product proves its role.
- Dialogue, voice-over, music, sound effects, and silence.
- Placement of overlays, captions, product artwork, and logo.

Use only the detail needed for the requested deliverable. Treat supplied and approved brand artwork as the source of truth. Do not ask a generative model to recreate exact logos, packaging text, or complex interface copy when it can be composited in post.

## Handoff to storyboard

Pass forward the verified product facts and evidence, audience and market, channel and language, duration and aspect ratio, premise, story beats, opening hook, payoff, visual and sound direction, assumptions, restrictions, and approved assets.
