# Category Playbooks

Use these as creative starting points when the category is known and the brief gives no stronger direction. They are not evidence for product claims. Verify every factual or regulated statement independently.

## Consumer packaged goods

Prioritize fast product recognition, usage occasion, sensory detail, and a memorable ritual. Show the real package and use context. Avoid generic happy-life scenes, invented ingredients or benefits, and unreadable packaging.

## Beauty and personal care

Use truthful skin, hair, texture, application, and sensory details. Build trust with clear use context and evidence. Avoid unrealistic transformations, medical promises, plastic-looking skin, and face or hand drift.

## Automotive and mobility

Use believable vehicle movement, interior detail, road context, and a clear reason for the product's presence. Respect safe driving behavior and local rules. Avoid impossible vehicle dynamics, unsafe driver actions, or unsupported performance claims.

## Consumer technology

Make one feature legible through a realistic use case. Use controlled product close-ups and only authentic interface references. Avoid fabricated UI, illegible screens, and feature claims without proof.

## Food and beverage

Prioritize appetite, temperature, texture, preparation, and social occasion. Use macro details and credible food behavior. Avoid artificial textures, package drift, and unmotivated lifestyle scenes.

## Internet services and apps

Start with the user's problem and show the service resolving a verified step. Keep interface choreography accurate and legible. Avoid invented screens, vague transformation claims, and over-explaining with subtitles.

## Public-interest and nonprofit work

Use emotional truth, restraint, dignity, and a clear behavioral action. Avoid melodrama, guilt manipulation, stereotypes, and imagery that undermines the people represented.

## Brand storytelling

Build a recognizable point of view, world, motif, or character tension. Keep the brand's role present in the idea, not only at the logo. Avoid beautiful but empty imagery and vague manifestos.

## Functional product demonstrations

Show the problem, the verified feature, visible proof, and the result in a simple sequence. Give the demonstration a human reason to matter. Avoid tutorial dryness, claim overload, or implying a result the product cannot guarantee.
