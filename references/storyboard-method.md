# Director-Level Storyboard Method

For cinematic TVC structures, multi-grid boards, product breakdown films, or continuous-shot prompts, also use `advanced-storyboard-and-film-structures.md`.

Turn an approved creative premise into a timed sequence that can be filmed or generated.

## Think before listing shots

Complete these steps first:
1. State the creative idea in one sentence.
2. Map the emotional progression from opening to payoff.
3. Define the protagonist, goal, situation, obstacle, product-related turning point, and resolution.
4. Choose a visual language: performance, framing, lens, camera movement, light, color, art direction, and editing rhythm.

Avoid empty style labels unless they are translated into visible choices. Every shot must advance the story, prove a necessary point, or deliver the payoff.

## Confirm format and timing

Carry forward known duration, aspect ratio, channels, dialogue or voice-over, overlay needs, product and logo requirements, and live-action or AI-generation mode. Ask only when an unknown changes the execution materially.

Set continuous timecodes from zero to the requested end. Shot durations must add up exactly, without gaps or overlaps. For a short video, the first three seconds need a legible hook; do not spend the opening on a slow establishing shot or logo.

## Write the shot sequence

For every shot, specify:
- Shot number, start and end timecode, and duration.
- Framing and concrete camera movement.
- Visible action, performance, and emotional change.
- Product state and its story or proof purpose.
- Dialogue, voice-over, on-screen text, and subtitles as applicable.
- Music, sound effects, silence, and transitions.
- The action, reaction, match-on-motion, sound, or visual motif that motivates the next shot.

Use one shot per table row. Keep actor, wardrobe, product, props, location, time, lighting, and color consistent. Mark the climax or key proof shot and the ending memory point when relevant. Show only product behavior supported by the brief.

## Logos, interfaces, and ending

Place exact readable text, logos, and complex interfaces in post-production unless approved artwork and a reliable production method are supplied. Do not invent product UI or brand typography.

Time the final story beat to the brief. If the current project specifies a separate end card, include its agreed content, placement and timing in the shot list.

## Quality check

Confirm that the storyboard is shootable or AI-generatable, the emotional arc is visible, every shot has a reason to exist, the story and product proof connect, timing is exact, continuity is trackable, and the ending is intentional.
