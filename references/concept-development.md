# Concept and Proposal Development

Use this stage to explain why a campaign idea fits the brief before converting it into shots.

## Proposal logic

1. Project understanding: brand context, product truth, audience situation, and communication objective.
2. Insight: the real psychological hinge or unmet need, stated specifically rather than as a category cliché.
3. Creative idea: one memorable sentence, key message, and approved slogan or campaign theme.
4. Concept: why this idea answers the communication task and why the audience can believe it.
5. Story outline: character or situation, conflict, turning point, product role, emotional landing.
6. Key visual: the image or visual metaphor that makes the idea recognizable.
7. Communication map: insight to theme to story to product proof to brand memory.
8. Deliverables: channels, lengths, formats, adaptations, and production text requested.

Keep this at concept level. Do not turn the proposal into a shot list unless the user requests a storyboard.

## Creative routes

When the user requests a proposal and has not selected a route, offer two or three meaningfully different routes. Each must differ in audience insight, product role, story logic, and video language—not merely adjectives. After the user selects a route, expand that route into the proposal; do not expand every option unless asked.

## References

Use real visual, campaign, or film references only when asked or when they answer a concrete question about mood, craft, category convention, or platform behavior. When a proposal needs references, gather three to eight relevant sources across image, video, campaign, or cinematography as appropriate. For each, include a title or source label, direct link, why it is relevant, and which proposal question it supports. Prefer sources the user can inspect. Do not imply a reference proves the user's product claim or copy the execution.

If browsing is unavailable, label references as pending and provide useful search terms rather than inventing examples.

## Handoff

After a route is selected, pass its communication logic, story outline, product role, key visual, tone, references, deliverables, and risks into the director-treatment or storyboard stage as requested.
